CHESS SCREENSHOT ANALYSIS

Open the Chess Screenshot Analysis desktop shortcut.
Click Preload for fastest results, and wait for Everything preloaded.
Copy a chessboard image, then press Ctrl+V in the tool.
Stockfish 19 starts automatically and shows up to four live lines.

The side whose pieces are at the bottom moves by default.
Change Side to move to override it. A new image returns to automatic detection.
Use Screenshot orientation if the detected board orientation is wrong.
Click a move in a line to preview that position.
Drag a piece, or click it and then its destination, to play a legal move.
The turn advances and Stockfish recalculates automatically after each move.
Undo move goes back; Reset position restores the original screenshot or FEN.
You can also play a move from a line preview to analyze that continuation.
Pawn promotion offers a choice of queen, rook, bishop, or knight.

The default search is five seconds. First results appear while it is searching.
Choose one second for quick checks, or a longer search for difficult positions.
Stop pauses the engine; Analyze again restarts it.

Preload warms the screenshot recognition model, its inference runtime,
the board assets, and Stockfish's built-in neural network. They remain ready
in memory while the tool and its local server are running. Reopening or
refreshing the page may require preloading the browser model again.

Screenshots do not reveal castling history or en passant rights.
Castling checkboxes for White and Black are guessed from the king and rook
starting squares. Uncheck rights if those pieces moved earlier, or use
Guess from board to infer them again. Played moves update rights automatically.
Check the recognized board, side to move, and Position FEN when needed.
Uncertain piece recognition asks you to check the board before analysis.

This tool runs locally at http://127.0.0.1:8765.
Images are processed locally and are not uploaded.
Engine: Stockfish 19, 12 threads, 1024 MB hash, full strength, four lines.
Recognition and the board interface build on Fenshot and cm-chessboard.
Their licenses remain in FENSHOT-LICENSE and node_modules.
